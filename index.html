    Sources

    • 2024 Housing Supply Gap Analysis, Bowen National Research for the NC Chamber Foundation, NC Home Builders Association and NC REALTORS: homes short, households, vacancy, list prices.
    • U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 2020 to 2024: rent, home value, income, renter costs, mobile homes, year built.
    • U.S. Census Bureau, Building Permits Survey, county files 2021 to 2025: permits.
    • NC Housing Coalition 2026 county profiles: fair market rent, housing wage, eviction filings.
    • NC DEQ Viable Utilities, February 2026 list: distressed water and sewer systems.
    • NC Department of Commerce 2026 development tiers; NC Rural Center density rule; NC regional councils of government.

    Definitions

    • Homes short: additional homes the Bowen study projects a county needs between 2024 and 2029.
    • Permits as a share of yearly need: average homes permitted per year in 2023 to 2025, divided by one fifth of the five-year gap.
    • Kind of county: rural is 250 people per square mile or fewer on the 2020 Census, regional city or suburban is 250 to 750, urban is more than 750.
    • Peers: the five counties of the same kind with the closest number of households.
    • Distressed system: a local water or sewer utility the state has designated as struggling to pay for upkeep and expansion.
    • Issues: screening checks on a county's numbers, using cut-offs chosen for this site; each states its cut-off.

    Limits

    • The gap was modeled before Hurricane Helene and does not count homes lost in western North Carolina.
    • A county where no permit office reports shows zero permits, so very low figures can reflect reporting.
    • Permits also replace demolished homes, so the pace figure is a rough check.
    • Survey figures for small counties have wide margins of error.
    • The issue checks sort counties for a first look and are not findings of the sources.
    • A blank in a project card means the source did not state the figure.
